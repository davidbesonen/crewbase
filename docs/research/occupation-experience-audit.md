# Crewbase occupation and experience audit

Reviewed 2026-09-24 against the local development catalog: **8 industries, 61 distinct occupations, 81 industry/occupation placements**. The industry membership comes from `db/catalogs/occupations_v2.rb`; the local inventory matched it. This audit does not add occupations. The redundancy review below now recognizes core duties from selected occupations.

## Decision and scope

Use selectable **work specialties** and **functional tool families** between an occupation title and a brand/model/individual technique. A useful option describes a responsibility someone can perform or a class of tools they have used, and can distinguish plausible candidates for a job. Keep products, versions, instruments played, genres, unusual methods and project examples in personal details.

The implemented vocabulary has **94 capabilities/services and 52 selectable equipment/software families**. All previous keys retain their meaning for compatibility. Twelve broad equipment/software umbrellas are retired from new selections; a distinct audio-workstation family separates recording/editing/mixing tools from notation software. An individual occupation surfaces a much smaller selection. The full catalog is not intended as an onboarding checklist.

The sources below establish work responsibilities and equipment/workflow distinctions. The final labels, grouping, parent relationships and occupation prompts are **Crewbase product-design judgments**, not a taxonomy endorsed by those sources. They need feedback from practicing users; research and tests do not establish measured recommendation quality.

## Industry review

- **Broadcast & Live Streaming:** distinguish on-air/editorial roles from engineering and playout. Live switching, camera shading, replay, graphics and remote feeds should not all be represented by generic video operation.
- **Corporate & Special Events:** cover both technical delivery and the event's running order, vendors, venue access and presentation content. LED, projection and playback are distinct selectable work.
- **Education & Training:** the maintained catalog has one role, Instructor. Differentiate course design, practical coaching, remote facilitation and assessment without inventing subject expertise.
- **Film, TV & Commercial Production:** preserve departmental boundaries between camera operation, focus, DIT, grip, electrics, sound, art, styling and production management. Senior titles do not confirm every departmental task.
- **Live Music & Touring:** distinguish FOH, monitors, systems, RF, playback, backline, lighting and travel logistics. A studio audio background alone must not satisfy a monitor engineer requirement.
- **Music Recording & Production:** distinguish recording, mixing, mastering, composing/arranging and music production; include the artist and catalog business work omitted by the original technical categories.
- **Theater & Performing Arts:** emphasize rehearsals, cue calling, costume changes, scenic work and live performance. The shared Technical Director title also has broadcast usage, so show the union of plausible work and require explicit selection.
- **Post-Production & Creative Services:** separate editing, grading, compositing, graphics and sound, while sharing media handoff/review concepts. Familiarity with editing software does not establish grading or compositing capability.

Shared occupation titles appear in more than one industry. The current profile model selects occupations, not a separate industry context for each role. Their prompts therefore combine plausible contexts. This is intentional: a Director or Technical Director selects the parts they actually perform. A future per-role context field could narrow the list further without changing category keys.

## Matching and rollout

- Selected occupations supply their conservatively mapped core duties for matching and search, without changing saved arrays. Additional specialties and all tools require explicit selections. Existing arrays, names, IDs and historical migrations remain intact; no new data migration is needed.
- A confirmed specialty covers its explicitly configured broader category only. Broad familiarity never implies a specialty, neighboring specialty, brand/model, qualification or equipment ownership.
- A job asking for both a family and its specialty counts the overlap once. Multiple independent specialties can still contribute separately.
- Employers can require work capabilities and tool families independently. Console familiarity alone does not prove monitor-mixing experience.
- Personal entries keep normalized exact-name matching. No product similarity, unrestricted semantic matching or prose inference is added.
- Search and both recommender directions share the same category relationships. Profile badges display actual selections, not silently added parent selections.
- Suggestions require user confirmation and omit core duties, retired umbrellas and redundant parents. Search in the form reaches other disciplines without resetting selections or reintroducing hidden core work.
- Marketing review: the existing claim about transferable capabilities, equipment families and optional specifics remains accurate; no new performance claim was added.

## Maintenance criteria

Add a category only when a credible hiring requirement cannot be expressed by an existing work specialty/tool family and optional detail. Reuse keys across industries. Keep existing key meanings stable, review parent links conservatively, and update the per-occupation prompts and tests together. Prefer a new reusable family over products, releases or individual techniques. Recheck this audit when adding an industry or occupation.

## Redundancy review

Every occupation now separates core duties from additional specialties. Actor includes acting/performance; FOH Engineer includes audience mixing; Colorist includes grading; Camera Operator includes operation/composition. These do not need another checkbox. The broader Audio Engineer title still asks about FOH, studio, location and other specialties. Technical Director only includes leadership across its different industry contexts; switching and staging remain explicit choices.

Core mappings express self-reported role experience, not qualifications or every possible specialty within a broad category. For example, Actor does not satisfy a specifically named voiceover requirement, and Gaffer does not establish any electrical license. Core duties disappear from matching when the occupation is removed unless separately selected.

The equipment audit retires overlapping umbrellas for audio, lighting, cameras, video/display, broadcast/streaming, communications, audio software, video software, design software, project/business software, rigging/staging and styling tools. Familiarity with functional families remains explicit: knowing someone's occupation does not identify every type of equipment they use. Saved umbrella selections remain visible and removable; narrower tool selections still satisfy older broad requirements, never the reverse.

The inventory below is the implemented product mapping, not a verbatim source taxonomy. It lists every current industry placement, its core duties, and suggested additional choices. Other non-core categories remain available across disciplines.
